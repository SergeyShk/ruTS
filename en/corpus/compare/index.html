    Corpus comparison¶

    compare_corpora(a, b, window=1000, features=None, labels=('A', 'B'), n_bootstrap=1000, seed=0, min_words=None)

    Compares two corpora of Russian texts by every feature of a text at once: which statistics tell authors, genres, translations, human and generated texts apart - and by how much. For single words keyness does the same, for the distances between texts - delta.

    The texts are cut in a row into windows of exactly window words by split_windows, so the windows of all texts have one length; an incomplete remainder at the end of a text and a text shorter than a window are dropped unless min_words allows shorter windows. The features of every window are computed by text_features or a function of one's own, and the tables of the two corpora are compared by compare_features of the anyTS core.

    Chekhov versus Tolstoy over the prose of the RussianLiterature dataset: 77 and 42 works, 249 and 1423 windows of 1000 words, under a minute.

    from ruts.corpus import compare_corpora
from ruts.datasets import RussianLiterature

rl = RussianLiterature()
chekhov = list(rl.get_texts(genre="prose", author="Чехов"))
tolstoy = list(rl.get_texts(genre="prose", author="Толстой"))

result = compare_corpora(chekhov, tolstoy, window=1000, labels=("Чехов", "Толстой"))

    Chekhov has several times more ellipses and exclamations per 1000 words, shorter sentences, a higher share of finite verb forms and a lower one of participles and converbs; Tolstoy is harder by every readability formula. Twenty works of Chekhov and one of Tolstoy are shorter than 1000 words and give no window, so the windows come from 57 and 41 works; with a smaller window they would be compared too.

    The p-values take the 1672 windows for independent (see the warning below): 90 of the 116 features have a corrected p-value below 0.01, but only 9 show a large effect by Cliff's delta. The interval of the difference of the medians resamples whole works and is the safer guide: it excludes zero for 67 of those 90 features, and for the length of a sentence it spans −5.4 to −1.2 words, where the windows alone would give −4.0 to −2.7. For the ellipses it spans 6 to 23 per 1000 words: the works of Chekhov differ in them a lot, on average from a fraction of one to 84 per 1000 words.

    Parameters¶

    Parameter Type Default Description
    a list[str] - Texts of the first corpus
    b list[str] - Texts of the second corpus
    window int 1000 Window size in words; None - whole texts
    features Callable None Text feature function; None - text_features
    min_words int None Smallest number of words in a window, at most window; None - a whole window, one word with window=None

    labels, n_bootstrap and seed go on to compare_features. compare_corpora checks them with check_comparison_params first, so a wrong name of a corpus or seed fails before any text is processed.

    Raises:

    • SourceTypeError - texts that are not a list of strings, features that cannot be called;
    • SourceError - a corpus without a window of min_words words or more;
    • ParameterError - a window or min_words that is not an integer of at least one, min_words above window, labels, a number of samples or a seed that check_comparison_params rejects.

    Features¶

    text_features(text)

    Returns 116 features of a text prefixed by source; an undefined feature is nan.

    Prefix Features Source
    basic_ shares of long, complex, simple, mono- and polysyllabic words, letters, spaces and punctuation marks; letters and syllables per word BasicStats
    readability_ all readability formulas and the consensus grade ReadabilityStats
    diversity_ lexical diversity measures DiversityStats
    morph_ shares of parts of speech and of single-valued features among the words (morph_pos_NOUN, morph_polarity_Neg, morph_style_variant_Short) and shares of values within the other features (morph_case_Gen, morph_tense_Past, morph_degree_Cmp; one share for a two-valued feature, morph_number_Plur) MorphStats by pymorphy3
    sents_ mean sentence length in words, standard deviation, coefficient of variation, autocorrelation of adjacent lengths (sentence_rhythm) - the rhythm of the text sentence_lengths
    punct_ frequencies of punctuation marks by type per 1000 words and the share of the letter ё punctuation_profile

    A 1000-word window takes about 0.02 s. Features over the spaCy parse (SyntaxStats, CohesionStats) are not in the default set - the function works on strings; they can be added with your own feature function via features, see the example below.

    The shares of spaces, letters and punctuation marks (basic_p_spaces, basic_p_letters, basic_p_punctuations) count characters as they are: line indents, double and non-breaking spaces in the files reflect the typesetting of the edition, not the text. In a corpus from different sources collapse them beforehand, for example re.sub(r"[^\S\n]+", " ", text).

    Parameter Type Default Description
    text str - Text string

    Raises:

    • SourceTypeError - a text that is not a string;
    • SourceError - a text without words.
    corpus_features(texts, window=1000, features=text_features, min_words=None)

    Returns the feature matrix of the windows indexed by (text number, window number), for your own classifiers as well; the level text of the index is what the bootstrap of compare_features resamples. Texts without words and windows shorter than min_words are skipped. compare_corpora is corpus_features for each corpus followed by compare_features; the split is needed when the features are computed once for several corpora and pairs have to be compared, for example all authors pairwise.

    Parameter Type Default Description
    texts list[str] - Texts of the corpus
    window int 1000 Window size in words; None - whole texts
    features Callable text_features Text feature function
    min_words int None Smallest number of words in a window, at most window; None - a whole window, one word with window=None

    Raises:

    • SourceTypeError - texts that are not a list of strings, features that cannot be called;
    • SourceError - a corpus without a window of min_words words or more;
    • ParameterError - a window or min_words that is not an integer of at least one, min_words above window.

    Your own features, for example syntactic ones by spaCy, are passed as a function; with the parse the same comparison takes about eight minutes:

    import spacy
from ruts import SyntaxStats
from ruts.corpus import compare_corpora, text_features

nlp = spacy.load("ru_core_news_sm")


def features(text):
    stats = SyntaxStats(nlp(text)).get_stats()
    return {**text_features(text), **{f"syntax_{key}": value for key, value in stats.items()}}


result = compare_corpora(
    chekhov, tolstoy, window=1000, features=features, labels=("Чехов", "Толстой")
)
result.filter(like="syntax_", axis=0)[columns].head(5).round(3)

    Comparison of feature tables¶

    compare_features(table_a, table_b, labels=('A', 'B'), n_bootstrap=1000, seed=0)

    Compares two corpora feature by feature. Each corpus is a table of the features of its windows, parts of the texts of about equal size, so the features do not depend on text length. The result has one row per column, by descending absolute Cliff's delta, the features without statistics last. The bootstrap resamples whole texts by the index level text; without it every row is a text of its own. Every feature has a generator of its own, from the seed and the name of the feature, so its interval does not change with the other columns. A column missing from one table gives nan.

    Parameter Type Default Description
    table_a DataFrame - Features of the windows of the first corpus, one row per window
    table_b DataFrame - Features of the windows of the second corpus
    labels tuple[str, str] ('A', 'B') Names of the corpora for the columns, two strings that give distinct columns
    n_bootstrap int 1000 Number of bootstrap samples
    seed int / Generator 0 Seed of the random number generator, a non-negative integer, or a numpy Generator; None - a random one

    A table that is not a DataFrame, repeats the name of a column or has a column that is not numeric raises SourceTypeError; the other parameters are checked by check_comparison_params.

    check_comparison_params(labels=('A', 'B'), n_bootstrap=1000, seed=0)

    Checks the parameters of compare_features before the texts are processed: it raises ParameterError unless the labels are two strings that give distinct columns (("diff", "B") would repeat median_diff), the number of bootstrap samples is an integer of at least one and the seed is None, a non-negative integer or a numpy Generator.

    Statistics¶

    For a feature with the values \(x_1 \dots x_{n_A}\) in corpus A and \(y_1 \dots y_{n_B}\) in corpus B (undefined and infinite values dropped; with fewer than two values on a side - nan):

    Column Description
    mean_A, mean_B, median_A, median_B means and medians
    median_diff, ci_low, ci_high the difference of the medians and its 95% percentile bootstrap interval: both sets are resampled n_bootstrap times (bootstrap_median_diff)
    cohen_d \(d = (\bar{x} - \bar{y}) / s\), \(s\) - the pooled standard deviation; 0.2 - a small effect, 0.5 - medium, 0.8 - large (calc_cohen_d)
    cliff_delta \(\delta = P(x > y) - P(x < y)\) from −1 to 1; \(\lvert\delta\rvert\) < 0.147 - a negligible effect, < 0.33 - small, < 0.474 - medium, large otherwise (Romano et al. 2006; calc_cliff_delta)
    auc the feature as a classifier: the share of the pairs of windows with the value in A greater than in B, ties counted as half; \(\delta = 2 \cdot AUC - 1\), 0.5 - the feature does not tell the corpora apart
    u, p_value the Mann-Whitney U statistic and the two-sided p-value (scipy.stats.mannwhitneyu)
    p_holm the p-value with Holm's correction for the number of features (holm_correction)
    n_A, n_B number of windows with a defined value
    n_texts_A, n_texts_B number of texts behind those windows

    The columns are anyts.corpus.COMPARISON_COLUMNS with the suffixes a and b replaced by the labels of the corpora. Cliff's delta and the AUC come from the same U statistic and agree; Cohen's d is sensitive to outliers and non-normality, so it is best read next to the delta.

    Windows of one text are not independent

    The test and the effect sizes take the windows for independent observations, which the windows of one text are not: with few texts the p-values are too small and reflect the chosen texts as much as the corpora. The bootstrap resamples whole texts (a cluster bootstrap), so its interval covers the spread between texts; it needs at least two texts on each side and is rough with few.

    Functions of the statistics¶

    The statistics of a row are available one by one from ruts.corpus:

    calc_cohen_d(values_a, values_b)

    Cohen's d with the pooled sample variances (ddof=1); nan with fewer than two values on a side or without spread.

    Parameter Type Default Description
    values_a list[float] - Values in the first corpus
    values_b list[float] - Values in the second corpus

    A value that is neither a number nor a missing one (None, NA) and values that are not a flat list raise SourceTypeError.

    calc_cliff_delta(values_a, values_b)

    Cliff's delta, the share of the pairs where the first value is greater minus the share where it is smaller; nan for an empty set or an undefined value. Computed by sorting, in \(O((n_A + n_B) \log n_B)\) time and linear memory.

    Parameter Type Default Description
    values_a list[float] - Values in the first corpus
    values_b list[float] - Values in the second corpus

    A value that is neither a number nor a missing one (None, NA) and values that are not a flat list raise SourceTypeError.

    bootstrap_median_diff(values_a, values_b, n_bootstrap=1000, rng=None, confidence=0.95, texts_a=None, texts_b=None)

    The percentile bootstrap interval of the difference of the medians. With texts_a and texts_b whole texts are resampled, the median of a draw taken over the values of its texts; with fewer than two texts on a side the interval is nan.

    Parameter Type Default Description
    values_a list[float] - Values in the first corpus
    values_b list[float] - Values in the second corpus
    n_bootstrap int 1000 Number of bootstrap samples
    rng Generator None Random number generator of numpy; None - a new one without a seed
    confidence float 0.95 Confidence level
    texts_a list None Texts of the values in the first corpus; None - every value is resampled on its own
    texts_b list None Texts of the values in the second corpus

    A value that is neither a number nor a missing one, values that are not a flat list and texts that are not labels of one type raise SourceTypeError; an n_bootstrap that is not an integer of at least one, a confidence that is not a number in (0, 1), texts not as many as the values and an rng that is not a numpy Generator raise ParameterError.

    holm_correction(p_values)

    Holm's correction for multiple comparisons: the p-values are sorted in ascending order, the i-th is multiplied by (m − i + 1), where m is the number of defined values, then the running maximum is taken and capped at one; nan stays nan.

    Parameter Type Default Description
    p_values list[float] - p-values

    A p-value that is neither a number nor a missing one and p-values that are not a flat list raise SourceTypeError, and a p-value outside [0, 1] ParameterError.

    from ruts.corpus import calc_cliff_delta, calc_cohen_d, holm_correction

round(calc_cohen_d([2, 4, 6, 8], [1, 3, 5, 7]), 3)
calc_cliff_delta([2, 4, 6, 8], [1, 3, 5, 7])

    Windows of a text¶

    split_windows(text, window=1000, min_words=None)

    Cuts a text into consecutive windows of exactly window words, so the features that depend on length are comparable between texts. The rest is a window only with at least min_words words (by default a whole window, so it is dropped). A boundary goes before the first word of a window and its opening marks; a symmetric mark, such as a straight quote or the dash of an aside, closes the previous window when glued to its last word or after a closing mark. Windows are stripped of whitespace; window=None gives the whole text. The words are those of WordsExtractor, and the marks those of ruts.constants: quotes, brackets and dashes open a phrase (OPENING_MARKS), the straight and the English quotes and the dashes are symmetric (SYMMETRIC_MARKS), and the final marks, the comma, the semicolon, the closing brackets and guillemet close one (CLOSING_MARKS).

    Parameter Type Default Description
    text str - Text string
    window int 1000 Size of a window in words; None - the whole text
    min_words int None Smallest number of words in a window; None - a whole window, or one word without a window

    A text without words or shorter than min_words gives an empty list.

    Raises:

    • SourceTypeError - a text that is not a string;
    • ParameterError - a window or min_words that is not an integer of at least one, min_words above the window.
    from ruts.corpus import split_windows

split_windows("Раз два три четыре пять", 2, min_words=1)
split_windows("Он сказал: «Иди домой». Потом он вышел из комнаты", 4)

    ['Раз два', 'три четыре', 'пять']
['Он сказал: «Иди домой».', 'Потом он вышел из']

    Rhythm of the sentences¶

    sentence_rhythm(lengths)

    Gives the rhythm of the sentences from their lengths in words: the mean sents_mean, the standard deviation sents_std, the coefficient of variation sents_cv and the lag-1 autocorrelation of neighbouring lengths sents_autocorr (Yule, 1939). The deviation needs two sentences, the autocorrelation three of unequal lengths, the coefficient of variation a mean above zero; otherwise, and without sentences, a feature is nan.

    Parameter Type Default Description
    lengths list[int] - Lengths of the sentences in words

    Raises:

    • SourceTypeError - lengths that are not a list of integers;
    • ParameterError - a negative length.
    from ruts.corpus import sentence_rhythm
