    Formulas of other languages¶

    UnadaptedReadabilityStats(source, sents_extractor=None, words_extractor=None)

    Readability formulas made for English, German or Italian, computed from the basic statistics of a text with the coefficients they were published with (Linsear Write in the form of textstat), without a calibration for its language. Their values compare texts of one language with each other; they are no grade levels of another language. A word without syllables (a number, a preposition of one consonant) counts as monosyllabic in FORCAST and the Wiener Sachtextformeln and as easy in Linsear Write, as in textstat. The data source is a text, a Doc object of spaCy or a ready BasicStats object, as for ReadabilityStats.

    Raises:

    • SourceTypeError - a source that is neither a string, a Doc nor a BasicStats object, basic statistics of another class, an extractor of another type, a string or a Doc for a class without basic_stats_class;
    • SourceError - a source without words or without sentences.

    The class extends the UnadaptedReadabilityStats of the anyTS core with the basic statistics of ruTS, which count the syllables and the letters of Russian words and take the Russian SentsExtractor and WordsExtractor by default. The print_stats table and the error messages are in Russian (UNADAPTED_READABILITY_STATS_DESC and CORE_MESSAGES of ruts.constants).

    The formulas are not calibrated for Russian. Their values serve to compare Russian texts with each other and with the results of these formulas in other tools, but they give no grade of the Russian school: on the first-grade texts of the TextsByGrade dataset the median FORCAST is 14.9, the Powers-Sumner-Kearl grade 8.2 and the Wiener Sachtextformel 1 9.4. These formulas therefore have no reading scales, describe gives None for them, and they do not enter the consensus grade. The formulas with coefficients for Russian are in ReadabilityStats.

    from ruts import UnadaptedReadabilityStats

text = "Ног нет, а хожу, рта нет, а скажу: когда спать, когда вставать, когда работу начинать"
urs = UnadaptedReadabilityStats(text)
urs.print_stats()

    Parameters¶

    Parameter Type Default Description
    source str / Doc / BasicStats - Data source
    sents_extractor SentsExtractor None Sentence extraction tool, not used for ready basic statistics
    words_extractor WordsExtractor None Word extraction tool, not used for ready basic statistics

    Attributes¶

    Attribute Type Description
    bs BasicStats Basic statistics of the text

    Ready basic statistics can be taken from a ReadabilityStats object, so as not to compute them again:

    from ruts import ReadabilityStats

rs = ReadabilityStats(text)
urs = UnadaptedReadabilityStats(rs.bs)
urs.bs is rs.bs, urs.forcast

    Metrics¶

    Every metric is a property of the object, computed on each access from bs, and get_stats gives them all. A hard word has complex_syl_factor syllables or more (3), a long word long_word_letter_factor letters or more (7).

    Metric Language and readers Range Higher means
    forcast English technical texts, a grade 5-20 Harder
    linsear_write English, a grade -1 and above Harder
    powers_sumner_kearl English primary school texts, a grade Any Harder
    eflaw English for foreign learners 1 and above Harder
    wiener_sachtextformel_1 ... wiener_sachtextformel_4 German non-fiction, a grade from 4 to 15 Any Harder
    dickes_steiwer German, without syllables Any Easier
    gulpease Italian, by letters 0-100 for most texts Easier
    fucks German, syllables per sentence 0 and above Harder
    strain_index English 0 and above Harder
    elf English texts read aloud 0 and above Harder
    wheeler_smith English primary school texts 0 and above Harder

    Here ASL is the mean number of words per sentence, ASW the mean number of syllables per word. The functions of the formulas are imported from ruts.unadapted_readability_stats and compute a value from counts:

    from ruts.unadapted_readability_stats import calc_forcast, calc_gulpease

calc_forcast(n_monosyllable_words=30, n_words=150)
calc_gulpease(n_letters=550, n_words=100, n_sents=8)

    FORCAST¶

    forcast · Caylor et al., 1973

    calc_forcast(n_monosyllable_words, n_words)

    The grade of Caylor et al. (1973) for technical texts: 20 minus a tenth of the monosyllabic words per 150 words, from 5 (every word monosyllabic) to 20; nan without words.

    \[ \textrm{FORCAST} = 20 - \frac{150 \times \textrm{Monosyllabic words}}{10 \times \textrm{Words}} \]
    Parameter Type Default Description
    n_monosyllable_words int - Number of monosyllabic words
    n_words int - Number of words

    A count that is not an integer or is negative, and more monosyllabic words than words, raise ParameterError.

    Linsear Write¶

    linsear_write

    calc_linsear_write(n_easy_words, n_hard_words, n_sents)

    The grade of Linsear Write in the form of textstat; the Lensear Write Formula of O'Hayre (1966) is another one, of the monosyllabic words and the sentences of a sample of 100 words. An easy word, of at most two syllables, weighs 1 and a hard one 3; their sum per sentence \(r\) is halved above 20, and otherwise loses 2 before it is halved. It is computed over the whole text rather than a sample of 100 words; nan without sentences.

    \[ r = \frac{\textrm{Easy words} + 3 \times \textrm{Hard words}}{\textrm{Sentences}}, \quad \textrm{LW} = \begin{cases} r / 2 & r > 20 \\ (r - 2) / 2 & r \le 20 \end{cases} \]
    Parameter Type Default Description
    n_easy_words int - Number of the words of one or two syllables
    n_hard_words int - Number of the words of three syllables or more
    n_sents int - Number of sentences

    A count that is not an integer or is negative raises ParameterError.

    Powers-Sumner-Kearl grade¶

    powers_sumner_kearl · Powers et al., 1958

    calc_powers_sumner_kearl(n_syllables, n_words, n_sents)

    The recalculation of the Flesch formula by Powers, Sumner and Kearl (1958) as a grade, for primary school texts; nan without words or sentences.

    \[ \textrm{PSK} = 0.0778 \times \textrm{ASL} + 4.55 \times \textrm{ASW} - 2.2029 \]
    Parameter Type Default Description
    n_syllables int - Number of syllables
    n_words int - Number of words
    n_sents int - Number of sentences

    A count that is not an integer or is negative raises ParameterError.

    McAlpine EFLAW¶

    eflaw · McAlpine, 2005

    calc_eflaw(n_mini_words, n_words, n_sents)

    The score of McAlpine (2005) for readers of English as a foreign language: the words and the mini words, of one to three letters (mini_word_letters), per sentence; 20 and below is very easy, 30 and above confusing. nan without sentences.

    \[ \textrm{EFLAW} = \frac{\textrm{Words} + \textrm{Mini words}}{\textrm{Sentences}} \]
    Parameter Type Default Description
    n_mini_words int - Number of the words of one to three letters
    n_words int - Number of words
    n_sents int - Number of sentences

    A count that is not an integer or is negative raises ParameterError.

    Wiener Sachtextformel¶

    wiener_sachtextformel_1 · wiener_sachtextformel_2 · wiener_sachtextformel_3 · wiener_sachtextformel_4 · Bamberger and Vanecek, 1984

    calc_wiener_sachtextformel(n_complex, n_long_words, n_monosyllable_words, n_words, n_sents, variant=1)

    The four formulas of Bamberger and Vanecek (1984) for German non-fiction, a grade from 4 to 15, over the percentages of the words of three syllables or more (MS), of more than six letters (IW) and of one syllable (ES) and the mean sentence length (SL); nan without words or sentences. A variant that is not an integer from 1 to 4 raises ParameterError.

    \[ \begin{aligned} \textrm{WSTF}_1 &= 0.1935 \cdot \textrm{MS} + 0.1672 \cdot \textrm{SL} + 0.1297 \cdot \textrm{IW} - 0.0327 \cdot \textrm{ES} - 0.875 \\ \textrm{WSTF}_2 &= 0.2007 \cdot \textrm{MS} + 0.1682 \cdot \textrm{SL} + 0.1373 \cdot \textrm{IW} - 2.779 \\ \textrm{WSTF}_3 &= 0.2963 \cdot \textrm{MS} + 0.1905 \cdot \textrm{SL} - 1.1144 \\ \textrm{WSTF}_4 &= 0.2744 \cdot \textrm{MS} + 0.2656 \cdot \textrm{SL} - 1.693 \end{aligned} \]
    Parameter Type Default Description
    n_complex int - Number of the words of three syllables or more
    n_long_words int - Number of the words of more than six letters
    n_monosyllable_words int - Number of monosyllabic words
    n_words int - Number of words
    n_sents int - Number of sentences
    variant int 1 Variant of the formula, from 1 to 4

    A count that is not an integer or is negative raises ParameterError.

    Dickes-Steiwer index¶

    dickes_steiwer · Dickes and Steiwer, 1977

    calc_dickes_steiwer(n_letters, n_words, n_sents, n_types)

    The short formula of Dickes and Steiwer (1977) for German, as Bamberger and Vanecek (1984) give it, over the letters per word, the words per sentence and the type-token ratio, without syllables; higher is easier. nan without words or sentences.

    \[ \textrm{DS} = 235.95993 - 73.021 \ln\left(\frac{\textrm{Letters}}{\textrm{Words}} + 1\right) - 12.56438 \ln\left(\frac{\textrm{Words}}{\textrm{Sentences}} + 1\right) - 50.03293 \cdot \textrm{TTR} \]
    Parameter Type Default Description
    n_letters int - Number of the letters of the words
    n_words int - Number of words
    n_sents int - Number of sentences
    n_types int - Number of the distinct words

    A count that is not an integer or is negative raises ParameterError.

    Gulpease index¶

    gulpease · Lucisano and Piemontese, 1988

    calc_gulpease(n_letters, n_words, n_sents)

    The index of Lucisano and Piemontese (1988) for Italian, by letters rather than syllables: about 0 for the hardest texts and 100 for the easiest, beyond them for extreme ones; below 80 a text is hard for a reader of primary school, below 40 for a reader with a secondary education. nan without words.

    \[ \textrm{Gulpease} = 89 + \frac{300 \times \textrm{Sentences} - 10 \times \textrm{Letters}}{\textrm{Words}} \]
    Parameter Type Default Description
    n_letters int - Number of the letters of the words
    n_words int - Number of words
    n_sents int - Number of sentences

    A count that is not an integer or is negative raises ParameterError.

    Fucks index¶

    fucks · Fucks, 1955

    calc_fucks(n_syllables, n_sents)

    The style characteristic of Fucks (1955): the mean word length in syllables times the mean sentence length in words, the syllables per sentence; nan without sentences.

    \[ \textrm{Fucks} = \textrm{ASW} \times \textrm{ASL} = \frac{\textrm{Syllables}}{\textrm{Sentences}} \]
    Parameter Type Default Description
    n_syllables int - Number of syllables
    n_sents int - Number of sentences

    A count that is not an integer or is negative raises ParameterError.

    Strain index¶

    strain_index · Nathaniel, 2009

    calc_strain_index(n_syllables, n_sents)

    The index of Nathaniel (2009): the syllables of three sentences over 10, here those of three sentences on average over the whole text; nan without sentences.

    \[ \textrm{Strain} = \frac{3 \times \textrm{Syllables}}{10 \times \textrm{Sentences}} \]
    Parameter Type Default Description
    n_syllables int - Number of syllables
    n_sents int - Number of sentences

    A count that is not an integer or is negative raises ParameterError.

    Easy listening formula¶

    elf · Fang, 1966

    calc_elf(n_syllables, n_syllabic_words, n_sents)

    The formula of Fang (1966) for texts read aloud: the syllables above one per word, per sentence, a word without syllables (a number) adding none; 12 and below is easy to listen to. nan without sentences.

    \[ \textrm{ELF} = \frac{\textrm{Syllables} - \textrm{Words of one syllable or more}}{\textrm{Sentences}} \]
    Parameter Type Default Description
    n_syllables int - Number of syllables
    n_syllabic_words int - Number of the words of one syllable or more
    n_sents int - Number of sentences

    A count that is not an integer or is negative raises ParameterError.

    Wheeler-Smith index¶

    wheeler_smith · Wheeler and Smith, 1954

    calc_wheeler_smith(n_polysyllable_words, n_sents)

    The index of Wheeler and Smith (1954) for primary school texts: the mean sentence length times the share of the words of two syllables or more, times 10, which makes 10 such words per sentence; it reads sentences where the original reads units ended by any final mark. nan without sentences.

    \[ \textrm{WS} = 10 \times \textrm{ASL} \times \frac{\textrm{Words of two syllables or more}}{\textrm{Words}} \]
    Parameter Type Default Description
    n_polysyllable_words int - Number of the words of two syllables or more
    n_sents int - Number of sentences

    A count that is not an integer or is negative raises ParameterError.

    Methods¶

    The methods get_stats, to_dataframe, print_stats and describe are those of the statistics interface of the anyTS core; the formulas have no scales, and describe gives None for any of them.

    from ruts import UnadaptedReadabilityStats

text = "Ног нет, а хожу, рта нет, а скажу: когда спать, когда вставать, когда работу начинать"
urs = UnadaptedReadabilityStats(text)
urs.describe("forcast"), urs.describe("gulpease")
urs.get_stats()
